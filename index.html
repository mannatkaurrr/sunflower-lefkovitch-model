01 About

About This Project

This project applies mathematical modelling and optimization theory to one of agriculture's fundamental challenges: how do you design a sunflower field to maximize seed yield? Rather than relying on trial-and-error, this work builds a quantitative framework grounded in plant biology, spatial geometry, and survival probability — producing field-ready recommendations from first principles.

The project focuses on Helianthus argophyllus (Silverleaf sunflower), a rare perennial native to the Texas coast, examining how floret arrangement, disc diameter, planting density, and row spacing interact to influence achene seed output. At its core is an elegant mathematical insight: sunflower florets naturally pack themselves according to the golden ratio (φ ≈ 0.618), and that biological logic can be harnessed to engineer optimal planting layouts.

02 Problem

Problem Context

Sunflower is a globally significant oilseed crop, with seeds carrying 40–65% oil content and 20–40% protein. Despite its economic importance, yield optimization remains highly empirical — farmers adjust spacing and density based on local intuition rather than predictive models. Existing research on maize, peach, and peanut crops demonstrates that spatial arrangement and density are among the most controllable yield levers, yet sunflower-specific optimization models are scarce.

Key challenges this project addresses:

  • How does disc diameter (flowering head size) determine maximum achievable seed count via the golden ratio?
  • What spatial planting layout — across a 10m × 10m field — maximizes the total number of flowering heads?
  • How does survival probability vary across lifecycle stages (germination → vegetative → flowering → harvest)?
  • Which species type (compact vs. tall) performs better under field area constraints?
03 Data

Data & Assumptions

Rather than relying on observational datasets, this project constructs a parametric model calibrated with biologically established constants. All parameters are sourced from peer-reviewed literature on Helianthus species and related crop science.

🌻

Two Species Simulated

Species A: disc diameter ≤ 10 cm. Species B: disc diameter 10–20 cm. Both expand to maximum diameter each season.

📐

Golden Ratio Packing

Floret arrangement follows φ = 360(2 − s) where s ≈ 0.618. Seed count derived from diameter via this relationship.

🌡️

Controlled Environment

Water, soil quality, temperature (25–30°C), and sunlight are held constant to isolate spatial effects.

📏

Fixed Field Size

Each simulated field is 10m × 10m. Two alternative spatial layouts compared per species type.

04 Methods

Methodology

1

Spatial Parameter Modelling

Built a geometric model to determine optimal row spacing and plant density for two sunflower species within a fixed 10m × 10m field. Compared tight vs. broad row configurations, drawing on literature showing that overly dense planting reduces per-plant head size and competes for root/light resources.

2

Golden Ratio Seed-Count Derivation

Applied the Fibonacci-based golden angle formula to translate disc diameter into maximum achene seed count. This mathematical relationship — observed in natural sunflower packing — is validated in the literature as an efficient bound for seed density per flowering head.

3

Life-Cycle Survival Probability

Modelled survival probability across five growth stages: germination, vegetative, reproduction, flowering, and harvesting. Computed cumulative survival rates to estimate the fraction of planted seeds realistically reaching harvest — a crucial multiplier for total yield estimation.

4

Sensitivity Analysis

Conducted sensitivity analysis on key parameters — disc diameter, plant spacing, and survival rates — to identify which variables most strongly drive total yield and where real-world uncertainty has the greatest impact on model predictions.

Key Findings

✓
Broad-row spacing outperforms dense configurations — wider inter-row spacing allowed sunflower roots and heads to reach full potential without resource competition, consistent with field studies.
✓
Species B (large disc) yields more seeds per plant, but Species A achieves higher total flowering-head count in the same 10m × 10m field due to smaller spatial footprint.
✓
Survival probability cascades significantly across growth stages — early-stage losses (germination, vegetative) have compounding effects on final harvestable yield.
✓
Sensitivity analysis identified disc diameter as the highest-leverage single parameter for per-plant seed count, while row spacing most strongly affects total field yield.

06 Impact

Impact & Applications

This project demonstrates that mathematical modelling can bridge the gap between biological theory and practical agricultural decision-making. The framework developed here is not limited to sunflowers — the parametric approach applies to any semelparous crop where spatial arrangement and seed packing geometry can be characterized.

🌾 Agricultural Planning

Field designers can use the spatial model to determine optimal planting configurations before sowing — reducing costly trial seasons.

🔬 Research Extensibility

The modelling framework can be extended to incorporate variable water availability, temperature gradients, or soil type — making it a foundation for richer simulation work.

📊 Decision Under Uncertainty

Sensitivity analysis reveals which parameters farmers should prioritize when field conditions deviate from ideal — translating academic findings into practical risk awareness.

🧮 Math in the Real World

Demonstrates how pure mathematics — the Fibonacci sequence and golden ratio — has direct, measurable consequences in biological systems and crop science.
