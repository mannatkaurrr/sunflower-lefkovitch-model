## 1 INTRODUCTION & BIOLOGY

The sunflower species (Helianthus Annuus) are members of the Asteraceae plant family which comprises over 100 species of perennial and annual cultivated herbs, native to North America.¹ Helianthus Agrophyllus known as Silverleaf sunflowers is a rare perennial herbaceous flower native to the coast of Texas. They are multi-stemmed flowers which grow between 6-15ft and have silvery-green foliage. They are usually harvested in the soil in the months of May to June and last until the end of August. Silverleaf sunflowers typically produce between one to five flowering heads in their lifetime. The flowering heads are heliotropic, meaning it rotates to face the sun and has 10-15, yellow to orange, petals making them highly attractive to predators.

Silverleaf sunflowers produce a disc of mature achene seeds surrounded by their petals and can reach up to 20 cm in diameter. Achenes are dry fruit seeds, known as their offspring, varying in colour, often dark with white stripes. Florets contain these achene seeds that are compressed and flat in shape on the disk of the flowering head.¹ The arrangements of the florets are laid out to maximize the plant's exposure to the sun to maximize the reproduction growth of achene seeds since they are semelparous plants.¹ Florets spiral filling each gap between themselves starting from the center of the flowering head to the edge of the disc. Sunflower gives birth to achene seeds which live until the following year that contain high percentages of oil (40-65%) and protein (20-40%). Sources can be extracted and be used for oil, grounded into nutritious powder, and eaten raw. A typical seedbed of sunflowers is best sustained at 25-30 degrees Celsius. The seed rate, spacing, and several sunflowers per hectare vary widely depending on soil, climate/irrigation, and temperature. Typically, sunflowers produce from 28,000 to 75,000 seeds per hectare. Their growth cycle is divided into five stages: germination, vegetative, reproduction, flowering, and harvesting.

Semelparous sunflowers should be planted 1 to 1 ½ inches deep in well-drained soil . Row spacing is critical in yield production to enhance overall growth . Sunflowers do not yield better in tight rows, according to studies; broad rows are favoured to provide culture and room for sunflower roots and heads to grow to their full potential. Sunflowers are known to be seeding rate sensitive. It has been demonstrated that a higher head size with a greater quantity of seeds per plant results in a thinner stem. The number of seeds required per individual plant to establish a sufficient stand is referred to as seeding. Seeding may be used to create plant patterns in a field.

### LITERATURE REVIEW

In recent years, several research have been created by employing plant modelling algorithms to promote population increase.

Ding W L et al. employed an optimization technique to model maize, soybean, and rice plants based on their spatial organization and physical properties to provide the optimal crop production. They discovered that photosynthesis was essential for achieving a high output yield. The quality of the leaves and fruits was considerably influenced by field size in conjunction with Photosynthetic Active Radiation (PAR). They determined that by maintaining their spacing broad and distributing more light, the population generated great quality fruit and leaves. More room resulted in enhanced growth and yield per unit area. However, their research was based on a plant model simulation combined with photosynthesis.

Bénédicte Quilot-Turion et al. designed a virtual model to optimize the parameters of a peach tree to improve its qualities. They studied the genetic prototypes of peach trees to illustrate a production system in which they tested virtual experiments to create an ideal peach tree type with improved characteristics. However, this research was based more on abstract biological properties that consisted of the psychological processes of peach plants.

Kurt C et al. experimented with twin-row and single-row spatial planting with peanut plants to perform the yield of peanuts. They reported that the twin-row planting pattern of peanut plants provided a double amount of pod yield as the traditional single-row. Their results have shown that it is essential to accommodate plant density with plant spacing. Their reports have demonstrated that lower density plants per unit area increased pods per plant. This ultimately led to more plants per unit area and increased water and sunlight resources competition.
